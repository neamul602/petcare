> ⭐ Primary target — the Owner whose repeat-booking-on-platform is the engine of the curated-trust moat

**Priority:** 🎓 PRIMARY

**Created:** 2026-08-30

## Profile Summary

Bibi is the late-30s Banani resident who has had Tito — her diabetic Shih Tzu — for four years, since before the diagnosis. She is dual-income, lives in an apartment that Tito has never had to share with a stranger, and is the person in her household who keeps the insulin schedule, the low-carb kibble tin, and the printed vet notes in a single drawer. She has never used a boarding service before; the WhatsApp vendor list her friends keep sharing is, to her, a list of strangers who do not know Tito's glucose curve. The first time she books through PetCare takes her about nine minutes end-to-end and ends with Tito returned with a documented glucose log and two photos of him sleeping on a clean rug. The second time, she taps "Book again" and the intake is already pre-filled. The third time, she does not need to ask anyone for a recommendation — she opens the app, and her first search already ranks providers by what they have demonstrably handled well for diabetic small dogs.

Bibi matters to PetCare because she is the only one of the three personas whose repeated in-platform action is what makes the moat a moat. Rashid's care-record reputation compounds only when Bibi keeps booking him through PetCare rather than through WhatsApp. Ayaat's dashboard tells a true story only when Bibi's re-bookings register on-platform. The platform exists to make Bibi's first search feel like leverage rather than labour — and to make her second booking faster than the first.

## Background

### Life Context

**Household:** Dual-income, BDT 80k+ household; she is the primary decision-maker for pet care. Lives in an apartment in Banani with her spouse and Tito.

**The Tito chapter:** Has had Tito since he was a puppy — four years. Tito was diagnosed diabetic eight months ago. The diagnosis changed Bibi's relationship with Tito's care: twice-daily insulin injections, low-carb kibble only, no table food, quiet environment, and a vet who knows his curve personally. Bibi keeps Tito's printed vet notes in a single kitchen drawer alongside his insulin.

**Previous trips:** When the family travelled previously, Bibi's mother watched Tito at home. This Eid, her mother is in Chittagong. There is no fallback.

**Travel pattern:** Annual Eid trip to Cox's Bazar; the rest of the year, weekend getaways to Sylhet or the Chittagong hill tracts. The next trip after Eid is already pencilled in.

### Decision-Making Style

**Trust over price:** When Bibi does not personally know a vendor, she ranks trust signals above price by a wide margin. She has turned down cheaper WhatsApp vendors because the only signal she could find was a five-star rating averaged across dogs and cats and "all kinds of pets."

**Information-density preference:** Bibi reads structured intake forms rather than DM-style back-and-forth. She prefers one well-designed five-minute form to a thirty-minute WhatsApp negotiation.

**Loyalty horizon:** Once a provider has handled Tito's diabetes well, Bibi is loyal for the next three to five bookings. She is not promiscuous in her trust; she is patient in building it.

## Current Situation

### The Daily Reality (Before PetCare)

**The search problem:**
- Tito needs to be boarded for 8 nights over Eid. There is no fallback at home. The WhatsApp vendor list her friends keep sharing has nine names on it.
- For each vendor, the only public signal is a five-star rating that averages across species and special-care needs. None of the ratings separate "handled a diabetic dog successfully" from "puppies loved it here."
- Bibi has no way to ask "have you handled a diabetic small dog before?" without a thirty-minute DM thread that may end in "yes we can do it" without proof.

**The re-explanation problem:**
- Every WhatsApp thread starts from zero. Bibi has typed Tito's insulin schedule, his dietary needs, his anxiety triggers, and his vet contact more times than she can count.
- The structured notes live in her phone, not in any vendor's record. The next provider will start from zero.

**The handover problem:**
- When Bibi drops Tito off with a WhatsApp vendor, there is no log. There is no daily update mechanism that survives intermittent connectivity. There is no admin escalation channel if Tito has a hypoglycemic episode at 3am.
- After the handover, Bibi's only signal is silence until pickup. "No news" reads as "bad news she is hiding."

**The skills and tools she already has:**
- A printed vet record she updates herself
- A phone full of Tito's glucose logs in a notes app
- A list of friends who keep sharing WhatsApp vendors

**The specific gap:**
- Bibi needs a surface that ranks providers by what they have demonstrably handled well for diabetic small dogs — not by what they self-declare.
- She needs a structured intake that pre-fills from Tito's saved profile on the second booking rather than re-asking every time.
- She needs daily visibility during the stay — a photo of Tito and a note about his glucose curve, not a thumbs-up emoji.
- She needs an escalation channel if something goes wrong at 3am.

## Driving Forces

### ✅ Top 3 Positive Drivers (What She Wants)

**1. Tier-A fit on her first search**
- **Detailed description:** When Bibi opens the app and types "diabetic small dog," the curated shortlist of 3–5 providers is already populated with providers whose actual care-history includes diabetic small dogs handled successfully. She does not have to ask "do you know how to handle this?" because the demonstrated-care-fit signal is on the profile before she clicks.
- **Why it matters:** The first search is the moment the curated-trust moat either functions or doesn't. If Bibi has to wade through twenty self-declared "yes we can handle diabetic pets" profiles to find the one that has actually handled one, the moat has not yet started compounding.
- **What success looks like:** Bibi's first search shows three to five providers ranked by what they have demonstrably handled. Her first tap is a provider who has handled at least two diabetic small dogs in the last twelve months, with verified reviews with photos. She books without a thirty-minute DM negotiation.
- **PetCare Promise:** Demonstrated Care Fit replaces self-declared Capability as the trust signal. FR-11 (curated 3–5 shortlist with capability-and-tier baseline), FR-21 (verified reviews with photos from the actual stay), FR-43 (daily updates with structured care-history records), FR-44 (per-provider species and special-care handling history), FR-49 (provider profile deepening through accumulated care-records).

**2. Daily in-stay visibility**
- **Detailed description:** During the stay, Bibi receives at least one update per day — a photo of Tito and a note about his glucose curve, his appetite, his behaviour. The updates are structured (photo + short note), auditable, and tied to the booking record. She does not have to chase the provider for "any news?" because the news arrives.
- **Why it matters:** "No news is bad news she is hiding" is the default anxiety Bibi carries from the WhatsApp market. The Daily Update is the structural answer to that anxiety — not by promising "everything will be fine" but by making the absence of news impossible to confuse with bad news.
- **What success looks like:** Each day of the stay, Bibi receives a structured update. The updates accumulate into the care-history record for Tito that survives the stay and pre-fills the next booking. The verified review at the end of the stay has two photos from the actual stay attached.
- **PetCare Promise:** Structured daily updates with photo + note, auditable by Admin, soft-enforced (reminders sent, not blocking completion). FR-1 (Owner Booking Lifecycle step 6 — in-stay updates), FR-15 (Daily Updates as a first-class surface during the stay), FR-26 (booking-scoped chat with audit), FR-43 (Daily Updates not blocking completion but accumulating into care-history), FR-45 (Acute Event posture as a launch-blocking surface).

**3. Second-booking speed**
- **Detailed description:** The second booking should take less time than the first. Bibi taps "Book again" on the favorited provider's last booking; Tito's full care history pre-fills the intake in seconds rather than minutes. The provider sees Tito's prior successful stay on the request — no re-litigation of capability. Both sides experience the moat functioning.
- **Why it matters:** The second booking is the moment the moat either compounds or breaks. If the second booking is harder than the first, Bibi will defect to WhatsApp for the third booking. If it is faster, she will stay because the on-platform repeat-value is now structurally better than the discount a WhatsApp DM would offer.
- **What success looks like:** Median time from "Book again" tap to Booking Request submission is under four minutes (UJ-4's climax beat). The structured care-history is the carrier; the demonstrated-fit ranking is the surface; the favorited provider is the natural default.
- **PetCare Promise:** Care History portability across bookings; On-Platform Repeat Value mechanisms; Book-again UX path. FR-7 (Care History portability), FR-8 (Care History pre-fills future intakes), FR-48 (On-Platform Repeat Value — favoriting, repeat-owner signals, second-booking discount-resistance), SM-15 (Second-Booking Time as a metric).

### ❌ Top 3 Negative Drivers (What She Fears)

**1. Tito's diabetes mishandled in an acute event**
- **Detailed description:** During the stay, Tito's glucose drops overnight. The provider panics, guesses, or messages Bibi at 3am without a runbook. The provider either gives Tito the wrong insulin dose or fails to escalate. The acute event becomes the trust-breaker that turns Bibi from a re-booker into a one-time customer who tells the story to every Banani dog owner she meets.
- **Why this terrifies her:** Tito's insulin curve is precise. The vet has explained what a hypoglycemic episode looks like. Bibi does not want a provider improvising under panic; she wants a provider who has handled this before, who has the vet contact on speed dial, who knows to escalate to Admin immediately, and who documents the event after. Anything less is, to Bibi, a medical emergency waiting to happen.
- **What failure looks like:** Tito returned with a glucose log that does not match the curve; an acute event that was not documented; a provider who says "we handled it, no worries" without showing what they actually did; a payout decision that quietly compensates Bibi without acknowledging what happened; a review from Bibi that future owners never see because the platform moderated it.
- **PetCare Answer:** Acute Event response posture as a launch-blocking surface. The Runbook is a defined artifact: trigger conditions, role split (Provider in-the-moment / Admin platform-coordination), Owner notification cadence, Vet-Clinic Directory usage, Payout / Dispute implications, and post-mortem practice. FR-15 (Daily Updates surface), FR-26 (booking-scoped chat with audit), FR-45 (Acute Event posture as launch-blocking), R-10 (acute events are launch-blocking). Validated by SM-19 (Acute-Event Post-Mortem Closure Rate) and SM-20 (Acute-Event Time-to-Admin-Page).

**2. Care-history loss in the WhatsApp handoff**
- **Detailed description:** Bibi books through PetCare, the stay goes well, the second booking is about to happen — and then the provider messages Bibi on WhatsApp offering a 15% discount direct. The temptation is real (savings), the friction is real (avoidance of platform commission), and the cost is invisible (the care-history stops accruing to PetCare, the demonstrated-care-fit signal weakens for every future owner, the platform's reason to exist starts to dissolve).
- **Why this terrifies her:** This is the moment the curated-trust moat either compounds or breaks. If Bibi takes the discount and moves to WhatsApp, she has voted with her feet that the on-platform repeat-value is not worth the savings. The next provider she books through PetCare will have a thinner profile than the first one did. Within six months, the platform's reason to exist is gone.
- **What failure looks like:** Bibi's second booking happens off-platform; the provider's care-record stops accumulating; the next owner searching for a diabetic small dog specialist sees fewer demonstrated cases; the platform's matching layer gets thinner; the moat starts dissolving quietly while every dashboard still looks healthy.
- **PetCare Answer:** On-Platform Repeat Value mechanisms that make staying on-platform more valuable than the discount. Favoriting, repeat-owner signals, Care History portability, structured discount-resistance, and soft-signal capture of off-platform defection attempts (Admin's defection-detection dashboard). FR-10 (Care History portability), FR-48 (On-Platform Repeat Value), FR-49 (provider profile deepening), FR-50 (off-platform soft-signal capture), A-14 (defection monitoring).

**3. Radio silence after handover**
- **Detailed description:** Bibi books, hands Tito over, then hears nothing until pickup. The provider does not post daily updates; the chat is silent; the only signal Bibi has is "no news," which her anxiety reads as "bad news she is hiding." When she picks Tito up, the only verification she gets is Tito himself — and Tito cannot tell her whether the stay went well or whether the glucose curve was followed.
- **Why this terrifies her:** "No news" is not neutral for an owner with a special-care pet. Silence is interpreted as either "nothing happened" (which she cannot verify) or "something happened and the provider is not telling me" (which she cannot disprove). Without structured updates, Bibi cannot tell which it is.
- **What failure looks like:** Bibi picks Tito up, asks "how did it go?", gets a generic "fine, no problems," has no documented evidence to confirm or refute that answer, and exits the platform with a trust signal that is unverifiable in either direction.
- **PetCare Answer:** Daily Updates as a first-class surface — photo + note, structured, auditable by Admin, soft-enforced (reminders sent, missed updates do not block stay completion but are visible to Admin). FR-1 (in-stay updates), FR-15 (Daily Updates as first-class surface), FR-26 (booking-scoped chat), FR-43 (Daily Updates accumulating into care-history), SM-9 (Daily-Update Posting Rate during Active Stays as a soft metric).

## What Bibi Needs to See on PetCare's Surface

**1. The First-Search Surface**
- A curated 3–5 shortlist default ranked by demonstrated-care-fit (capability-and-tier at launch, demonstrated-fit at maturity)
- Per-provider species and special-care handling history visible before she clicks
- Verified reviews with photos from the actual stay, not just text-only ratings
- Launch Neighborhood density filter so she only sees providers who can actually serve Banani

**2. The Care History Surface**
- Tito's full care-history pre-fills on every new booking request — insulin BID, low-carb kibble, no table food, anxiety triggers, vet contact
- The provider sees the relevant history on incoming requests — no re-litigation of capability
- The history survives across bookings and across providers — it is the platform's record, not the provider's

**3. The In-Stay Surface**
- Daily Updates arrive without her asking — photo + note, structured, auditable
- Booking-scoped chat with Admin-audit available if the provider's responses feel off
- Acute Event Runbook visible — what triggers escalation, who to call, how payout is handled

**4. The Verified Review Surface**
- Reviews are gated by completed booking, one review per booking
- Reviews may include rating, text, and photos from the actual stay — the differentiator
- Reviews are public; admin moderation exists for cause
- The provider's reputation is built on what they have demonstrably handled well over time — species, special-care needs, repeat-owner success

**5. The Second-Booking Surface**
- "Book again" tap on the favorited provider's last booking
- Care History pre-fills in seconds rather than minutes
- The provider sees Tito's prior successful stay on the request — no re-litigation of capability
- Median time to confirmation under four minutes (UJ-4 climax)

**6. The Off-Platform-Defection-Resistance Surface**
- When the provider messages Bibi on WhatsApp offering a 15% discount, Bibi sees the on-platform repeat-value tools (favoriting, repeat-owner signals, care-history portability) and decides that staying on-platform is structurally more valuable than the discount
- The decision is logged as a soft signal in Admin's defection-detection dashboard — the moat stays observable
